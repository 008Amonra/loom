Loom: The Word That Became a Token, Twice

A four-letter English word that predates every company on this page now names two dead projects, a decommissioned blockchain, and an unknown number of meme coins.

A search for the word “loom” returns a project page, a static site, a yarn brand, and, lately, something new: “LOOM → Pump.swap.” A Solana meme token. The immediate reaction is a small thrill, followed immediately by the only question that matters here: which LOOM?

There is no “the” LOOM. There are many.

The First Loom

The original LOOM belongs to Loom Network, founded in 2017. It raised $25 million in a private sale in January 2018 — 22,500 LOOM per ETH, roughly seven cents a token. It built Basechain, a Delegated-Proof-of-Stake sidechain, and with it a genuinely famous product: CryptoZombies, the interactive Solidity tutorial that taught hundreds of thousands of developers to write smart contracts. Axie Infinity ran on the Loom SDK. Zombie Battleground raised a Kickstarter round and sold two million cards.

The LOOM token peaked near $0.77 in January 2018.

Then the market turned, the chain’s premise aged, and Basechain was decommissioned to free resources for a zkLoom protocol that remains, at this writing, mostly a testnet. The token that once backed a production network now changes hands for cents. That is the first Loom: a real product that outlived its moment.

The Factory

The second Loom is not a project. It is a byproduct of a machine.

Pump.fun launched in January 2024 and has since minted more than 11.9 million tokens — at peak, roughly 42,000 per day, close to one every two seconds, at times 83% of everything minted on Solana. No code, no presale, no team allocation: you type a name, pay a fee, and a coin exists. Price runs up a bonding curve. If enough people buy, the token “graduates” to PumpSwap, the platform’s own exchange, and trades there like any other asset.

In that factory, a word like “loom” is not a coincidence. It is a production run.

The Math of No Original Names

There are 456,976 possible four-letter strings (26^4). Pump.fun has minted 11.9 million tokens. If names were drawn uniformly at random from that space, collisions would be not just likely but mathematically certain — the birthday paradox at industrial scale, with the collision probability rounding to 100% before you finish reading this paragraph.

Model it on English words instead: there are roughly 50,000 common words, and if token names were spread evenly across them, the expected number of tokens called “loom” would be about 238. The remarkable thing is not that a “Loom” coin exists. The remarkable thing would be if there were only one.

There is not one. Trackers list several distinct Solana LOOMs with different contract addresses — one pool holding 924 million tokens worth about $7,600, another at a price so low one screener displays its “change since launch” as plus 3,297,102 percent. A percentage that large is not a return. It is a rounding artifact of a price that started at essentially nothing and never became anything else.

The Coincidences We Insist On

Humans will read meaning into this. Here are the coincidences available, in order of how much they cost you:

  • A developer’s own project, named “loom” years ago, now shares its name with both a 2018 token and a 2026 meme. A name collision with a ten-year-old project — and independently with a ten-month-old one.
  • The word “loom” means a weaving machine. These tokens weave nothing. The meaning is the product’s entire content.
  • The day the discovery was made, the same conversation had covered another collapsed token. That is not a signal. It is survivorship bias: the conversation about dead coins is where you find other dead coins.

None of this is meaningful. That is the point.

The Lesson

A system that mints eleven million tokens has exhausted the supply of original names. Every common word — and most uncommon ones — is now a ticker, and a decommissioned chain, and a meme, and a scam that impersonates one of the others. If you own a product name, it is already a coin. If you search for your own brand, you will eventually find a contract address wearing your clothes.

The original LOOM was a network. The new LOOM is a name. The difference between them is the entire story of this market: in 2018 you could still build something and name it; in 2026, the name is the whole product, minted at one every two seconds.
