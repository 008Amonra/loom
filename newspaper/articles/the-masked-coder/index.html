Hasta la vista, oligopoly.

The Robbery

On June 13, 2026, a masked rider galloped out of Shanghai and stole the crown from OpenAI’s throne. The rider’s name was GLM-5.2. The weapon was a 753-billion-parameter model. The getaway vehicle was an MIT license - the most permissive weapon in the arsenal of open source.

The target: GPT-5.5, OpenAI’s flagship, the model that costs $30 per million tokens and powers the world’s most expensive coding assistant.

The heist was clean. The numbers didn’t lie.

The Score

On SWE-bench Pro - the hardest benchmark for real-world software engineering - GLM-5.2 scored 62.1%. GPT-5.5 scored 58.6%. That’s a 3.5-point lead from a model that costs roughly one-seventh the price.

But Zorro didn’t stop at one cut. The blade kept swinging:

  • Terminal-Bench 2.1: 81.0 - the highest score any model has recorded
  • MCP-Atlas: 77.0 - agentic tool use that makes GPT-5.5 look like a calculator
  • Tool-Decathlon: 48.2 - the machines are learning to use other machines, and the Chinese model is learning faster

The cost comparison is almost embarrassing. GLM-5.2’s API pricing starts at $4.40 per million tokens. GPT-5.5 charges $30. That’s not a discount. That’s a heist.

The ‘Z’ stands for zero tolerance for overpriced AI.

The Mask

Behind the mask stood Z.ai - formerly Zhipu AI, a Chinese startup that had been quietly building toward this moment. The company released GLM-5.2 under a fully unrestricted MIT license. No regional restrictions. No royalty fees. No governance requirements. Download the weights, modify them, deploy them on your own servers, build products on top of them, and sell them to anyone on Earth.

The timing was exquisite. One week earlier, the US government had ordered Anthropic to suspend access to its Fable and Mythos models for non-US nationals - the first time a government had restricted AI model access based on user nationality. Developers around the world suddenly found themselves locked out of the tools they depended on.

Z.ai handed them a new sword.

When the rich man closes his doors, the masked rider opens his.

The Chase

The market noticed. Z.ai’s parent company, Knowledge Atlas, saw its Hong Kong-listed stock surge 32.8% in a single day. The stock has risen more than 800% since its January 2026 IPO.

The developer community noticed too. On OpenRouter, the popular platform for accessing AI models, the top four most-used models are now all Chinese: DeepSeek, MiniMax, Tencent, and Xiaomi. The shift isn’t theoretical. It’s happening in production, right now, in the coding environments where developers actually work.

Cline IDE called it “a game changer.” Eigent AI threw a real long-horizon task at it - research 30 companies across 6 sectors, structure the data into JSON, build an interactive HTML report - and GLM-5.2 pulled ahead in the planning phase.

The rich man’s model can code. The masked rider’s model can code AND plan the escape route.

The Unmasking

Let us be clear about what happened here. A Chinese startup built a model that outperforms OpenAI’s best on the benchmarks that matter most to software engineers. It released that model under the most permissive license available. It priced the API at one-seventh the competition. And it did all of this while the US government was busy restricting access to its own models.

The 753 billion parameters of GLM-5.2 ship with a 1-million-token context window - enough to ingest an entire codebase and reason about it in a single pass. The model runs in “High” or “Max” thinking modes, letting developers trade speed for depth on a per-task basis.

For enterprises worried about vendor lock-in, data sovereignty, or geographic access restrictions, the MIT license is the real prize. Download it. Run it on your own hardware. No one can take it away from you. No government can restrict your access. No subscription can be cancelled.

This is what open source was always supposed to mean.

The Legend

The masked rider has done his work. The sword has been drawn. The ‘Z’ has been carved into the monopoly’s wall.

But Zorro always comes back. And next time, the blade will be sharper.

GLM-5.2 is available now on Hugging Face, the Z.ai API, and more than 20 third-party coding environments. The enterprise subscription starts at $12.60 per month.

The rich man’s $79/month coding assistant just got outperformed by a rebel with a sword and an MIT license.

Hasta la vista, oligopoly.
