May the Automation Be With You?

AI promises to automate everything. Most things resist.

Every week someone discovers that AI can write an email, generate an image, or summarize a meeting. Every week they conclude that everything else will follow. It does not.

What Actually Automates

Automation works best on tasks that are repetitive, structured, and low-stakes.

Repetitive means you do the same thing more than three times a week. Structured means there are clear inputs and outputs. Low-stakes means a mistake costs you five minutes, not five thousand dollars.

Examples that work:

  • Data entry. Copy information from a form into a spreadsheet. AI reads the form, fills the cells. Done.
  • Email triage. Sort incoming messages by priority. Flag urgent ones. Archive noise.
  • Report summaries. Take a 40-page document, extract the three things that matter.
  • Code generation. Write boilerplate functions, tests, SQL queries. The boring parts.
  • Image generation. Product mockups, social media posts, concept art. Not final deliverables.

These are real. They save real time. They work today.

What Resists Automation

Everything else resists. Specifically:

Creative judgment. You can generate 100 logos in an hour. Choosing the right one takes a human who understands the client, the brand, and the context that no prompt captures.

Relationships. AI can draft a client email. It cannot read the room when the client is upset but not saying so. It cannot notice that the real objection is not about the deliverable.

Edge cases. Automation handles the happy path. The moment something unusual happens - a file in the wrong format, a client who uses a nonstandard workflow, a regulation that changed last month - the automation breaks and a human fixes it.

Ethics and judgment calls. Should we publish this? Is this ad misleading? Is this hire the right fit? These require values, not just patterns.

The Automation Gap

McKinsey estimates that generative AI could automate 60-70% of current work activities. This number is true and misleading in the same way that “60% of the Earth is water” is true and misleading. The 60% is mostly shallow water. The deep work - the judgment, the creativity, the relationships - remains human territory.

The real number that matters is not what percentage of tasks can be automated. It is: how much of your specific job can be automated without degrading the quality of the output?

For a data entry clerk: most of it. For a therapist: almost none. For a developer: the boring parts, which are still valuable. For a journalist: the research. Not the reporting.

What This Means for Small Businesses

If you run a small business, here is the honest assessment:

Automate the plumbing. Invoicing, scheduling, data entry, email sorting, social media posting. These are solved problems. Stop doing them by hand.

Augment the craft. Use AI to draft, brainstorm, and generate options. Then apply your judgment to select and refine. The AI is a faster draftsman. You are still the architect.

Never automate trust. Client communication, partnerships, hiring, pricing decisions. These require human judgment and human accountability. If you automate them and something goes wrong, you cannot blame the AI. You hired it.

The Star Wars Lesson

The Force was powerful. It did not replace skill, strategy, or sacrifice. Luke still had to fly the ship. Leia still had to make the call. Yoda still had to train the student.

AI is the same. It amplifies. It does not replace. The people who understand this will build things that matter. The people who do not will spend their time fixing broken automations and wondering why the promised future did not arrive.

May the automation be with you. But do not mistake it for the whole force.
