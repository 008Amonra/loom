GLM-5.2 vs GPT-5.5: The Open-Source AI Race Heats Up

When OpenAI released GPT-5.5 last month, the usual fanfare followed. Headlines proclaimed a new era. Benchmark scores climbed. Stock tickers danced.

Then a Chinese AI lab named Zhipu dropped GLM-5.2.

The model is open-weight. It runs on 8x H100 GPUs instead of 64. It costs 1/7th to train. And on most benchmarks, it beats GPT-5.5.

The Benchmark Reality

Let’s skip the marketing. Here’s what the numbers actually show:

  • HumanEval (code): GLM-5.2 scores 94.2% vs GPT-5.5’s 91.8%
  • MMLU (knowledge): GLM-5.2 scores 88.1% vs GPT-5.5’s 87.3%
  • GSM8K (math): GLM-5.2 scores 96.7% vs GPT-5.5’s 95.2%
  • MATH (competition): GLM-5.2 scores 73.4% vs GPT-5.5’s 71.8%

Not a blowout. But consistent.

The Cost Equation

GPT-5.5 reportedly cost $120M+ to train. GLM-5.2 cost roughly $17M.

That’s not a typo.

The efficiency gains come from architectural improvements - better attention mechanisms, smarter data curation, and a training recipe that maximizes compute utilization.

The Open-Weight Advantage

Here’s what matters for developers: GLM-5.2 weights are publicly available. GPT-5.5 is API-only.

You can:

  • Run GLM-5.2 locally
  • Fine-tune it for your use case
  • Deploy it without per-token fees
  • Audit it for bias or security issues

You can’t do any of that with GPT-5.5.
