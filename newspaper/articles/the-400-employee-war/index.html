The 400-Employee War

Apple sues OpenAI over trade secrets. OpenAI publishes iMessage screenshots in its defense. More than 400 former Apple engineers now work at OpenAI. The lawsuit is about hardware, but the real fight is about who gets to own the future of personal computing.

In July, Apple sued OpenAI. On Monday, OpenAI published a blog post titled “Apple is getting this wrong,” complete with internal iMessage screenshots, and called the lawsuit “careless, aggressive and oddly personal.”

This is not how trade secret disputes usually go.

The Accusation

Apple’s original complaint, filed July 10, alleges that former Apple engineer Chang Liu and former Apple design executive Tang Tan ran a scheme to funnel confidential product information to OpenAI’s hardware business. Tan spent 24 years at Apple before joining io Products, the hardware startup he co-founded with Jony Ive, which merged with OpenAI in July 2025.

Apple claims Liu and Tan extracted confidential files before and after their departures. The company is seeking a preliminary injunction to bar OpenAI from accessing, using, or disclosing the alleged information. A hearing has not yet been scheduled.

More than 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI. Apple did not name Jony Ive in the suit, which Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman described as likely deliberate, given Apple’s relationship with Laurene Powell Jobs.

The Rebuttal

OpenAI’s response was unusual for a corporate legal dispute: a public blog post with receipts.

The company published iMessage exchanges showing Apple employees repeatedly asking Liu for help locating files and technical information after his January 22 departure, including directing a colleague to AirDrop files from his Apple-linked iCloud account. The same messages show an Apple employee still asking Liu about internal files as late as March 5, with another participant in the thread calling the exchange “highly irregular.”

OpenAI also published the email chain from Apple’s pre-litigation contact, showing that Apple’s outside counsel had emailed the wrong person in February after confusing two Asian last names. The company said it heard nothing further about the specific allegations for five months before Apple filed suit.

“Tang has always been clear with the team that we do not want, and must not use, any confidential information from other companies,” OpenAI stated.

What This Is Really About

Apple makes hardware. OpenAI is building hardware. The two companies are now competing for the same thing: the device that sits in your pocket, on your desk, or on your face, and runs AI natively.

The lawsuit names two individuals, but the subtext is structural. Apple’s competitive advantage has always been vertical integration — designing the chip, the hardware, the operating system, and the AI stack in a single building. If OpenAI is building its own consumer device with 400+ engineers who know how Apple does it, that advantage narrows.

The iMessage screenshots are a deft move. They shift the narrative from “OpenAI stole our secrets” to “Apple’s own people couldn’t stop asking our employee for help after he left.” Whether that holds up in court is a different question than whether it holds up in public opinion.

Neither company has commented on a settlement timeline. The hearing on Apple’s injunction request is pending.
