Attack tools now run around the clock and reason for themselves. They fingerprint your router, your IoT boxes, your open ports - and they try everything, at machine speed, without ever getting tired. One security firm logged over half a million probes a day against a single ordinary home connection. This traffic is not personal. It is not revenge. It is simply the cost of being online in 2026.

What they want
Not your homework. Your identity, your bank, your inbox.

Routers and IoT boxes are the favourite first step: old firmware, no updates, default passwords. A compromised router sees everything - your banking sessions, your email, your passwords - before they even leave the house.

Credentials are the real loot. Stolen logins are sold by the batch, and a single reused password across accounts is enough to start the dominoes. Recent attack waves even abuse AI chatbots to hand-deliver malware: a "helpful" custom GPT that quietly drops a remote-access trojan onto the machine that asked it for help.

And the machines that are not looted directly are often recruited - silent members of a botnet, burning your bandwidth and your electricity inside someone else's attack.

Why DIY fails
The guides are right. Nobody finishes them.
The fragment problem
Firewall from one tutorial, AppArmor from another, kernel settings from a forum post from 2019. Ten tabs, three sudo prompts, one dented confidence.
The verify problem
"I think I enabled it." A filter set but never checked is a filter that may be off. Guides teach the command, not the proof.
The fear problem
The right fix for a listening port is scary: it touches the network, it needs sudo, it could lock something out. So it stays open.

The answer is not willpower. It is a TODO list written by someone who has done it a hundred times, and - in the hardening tier - a pair of hands that already pressed the scary buttons.

What locked down actually means
Measurable, not magical

A hardened machine looks like this: firewall denies everything incoming by default, services bind only to loopback, SSH reachable only from your own network or VPN, automatic updates on, kernel hardening active, and a check + verify report that proves each line. Nothing is "trust me". Everything is "look". That is the whole pitch.

FAQ
Asked, answered
I do not think I am interesting enough to attack.
You are right, and you are missing the point. The attackers are not interested in you. They scan everything and attack what is open. Being uninteresting is not protection; being closed is.
Is the free check safe to run?
Yes. ./sec-toolkit.sh check is read-only and needs no sudo. It inspects firewall state, services, and settings, and changes nothing. Hardening is a separate, explicit, sudo step.
Will this break my computer?
Conservative defaults, a system snapshot before any change, and a restore path. That is the security mind-set applied to the process itself.
Is this only for Linux?
Linux is our standard - desktops and servers (Ubuntu, Pop!_OS, Debian). macOS and Windows are available on request, done hands-on with a restore point first.
